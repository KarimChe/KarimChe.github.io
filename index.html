Karim Cherifi

Junior Professor (CPJ) in Physics-Informed AI for Complex Technical Systems
SUPMICROTECH, FEMTO-ST, Besançon, France

My research focuses on data-driven modeling, physics-informed machine learning, and model reduction. In recent years, I have been particularly active in modeling port-Hamiltonian (pH) systems and in developing machine-learning frameworks that incorporate physical structure and prior scientific knowledge. I have also been involved in multiple industrial projects on modeling for digital twins, and I co-edited a book on physics-based and data-driven modeling for digital twins.

Thanks for stopping by! This website gathers information about my research and teaching activities. Feel free to explore, and don't hesitate to get in touch.

Research Interests

Physics-Informed Learning & Identification →

Structure-preserving learning of dynamical systems: pH neural networks, Kolmogorov-Arnold networks, diffusion models, neural scaling laws, greedy data collection and benchmarks.

Port-Hamiltonian Systems Theory →

Relations between port-Hamiltonian, passive, positive-real and dissipative systems; descriptor, discrete-time and time-varying settings; nearest scattering passive systems.

Model Order Reduction →

Minimal realizations, matrix-polynomial and solvent-based reduction, rational interpolation of parametric systems, battery models.

Digital Twins & Applications →

Hierarchical modeling of digital twins for electrical machines and large drives, flexible structures, power-system security control.
